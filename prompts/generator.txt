R' is a search hypothesis, not a replacement task. It was produced by
deliberately editing the representation of the problem above: an assumption
may be negated, an entity retyped, a predicate reified, a goal replaced by
one borrowed from a neighbouring domain. None of that changes what you have
to deliver.

What R' is for. It names a route. Reading the problem through a negated
assumption, a shifted type or an imported frame is a way of reaching a line
of attack you would not have taken from the statement alone. Follow it as far
as it is useful.

What you must deliver. An answer to the problem exactly as it appears in
<problem>. Before you commit to a final answer, check it against every
constraint in the original statement -- including any constraint that the
edit in R' relaxed, negated, dropped or replaced. The verifier V_P is bound
to the original problem P and is never applied to the edited formulation, so
an answer that is correct only for the edited version scores zero.

If the route through R' leads somewhere that solves only the edited problem,
say so in one line and then solve the original problem directly.
