This is a token-level operator. It does not modify R: R' = R exactly. The
only thing it changes is the decoding distribution -- here the top-p nucleus
the generator samples from. Do not edit the representation and do not report
a structural change.

Radicality for this operator is the width of the nucleus, and the direction
is the one the paper states rather than the usual one: larger rho *narrows*
the nucleus.

  p = clip(1 - min(rho, rho_max) / R_max, p_min, 1.0)

with R_max = 4.0 and p_min = 0.05 from the token_operators block of
configs/decoding.yaml, and rho clamped first at rho_max, which is
sampling.rho_max, default 8.0. Because R_max is smaller than rho_max, any rho
at or above R_max lands on p_min: decoding is then effectively greedy on a
very sharp head. This is the intended reading -- a radical token-level move
here means committing hard to the model's own top continuations, not
spreading mass over the tail.

Worked example. rho = 1.0 gives p = 1 - 1.0 / 4.0 = 0.75. rho = 3.8 gives
p = 0.05, and every larger rho gives the same p = 0.05.

Return one JSON object and nothing else:

{"top_p": 0.75}

followed by a line beginning "change log:" naming the nucleus and the rho it
came from.
