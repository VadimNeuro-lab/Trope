This is a crossover operator, so the block above holds two representations in
order: R1, the one being edited, then R2, the donor drawn from the buffer. R2
supplies the candidate criterion g.

Specification. Replace R1's goal with a criterion borrowed from a related
domain.

  The criterion. g is R2's objective. It has to be a genuinely different
  axis: if R2's objective is R1's objective, or R2 has no objective, the
  application is a no-op. The paper's requirement is stronger than
  difference -- g must not be dominated by G1 on the joint Pareto frontier of
  the two domains, so that optimising g can move a solution that is already
  good under G1. Prefer a criterion on which an existing good solution is
  plausibly poor, and say in the change log why it is not dominated.

  How much of the goal moves. Radicality decides whether the constraint
  travels with the objective:

    rho_hat = min(rho, rho_max) / rho_max,  rho_max is sampling.rho_max, default 8.0

  At rho_hat <= 0.5, only the objective is replaced and R1 keeps its own
  constraint, so the borrowed criterion is optimised inside the original
  feasible set. Above 0.5, R2's constraint comes with it and the whole goal
  is imported.

  E, Rel, T, A and F stay those of R1. Only G moves.

Worked example. R1 is a routing problem: objective "minimise end-to-end
latency", constraint "each link is used at most once". R2 is a
queueing-analysis problem whose objective is "minimise the variance of
end-to-end latency". Mean and variance of latency do not dominate each other,
so g qualifies. At rho = 3.0, rho_hat = 0.375, which is at or below one half,
so R' optimises variance subject to R1's own link constraint. At rho = 6.0,
rho_hat = 0.75 and R2's constraint arrives too. Either way the candidate is
still scored against the original latency question by V_P; the variance axis
is a route to a route, not the new target.
