This is a crossover operator, so the block above holds two representations in
order: R1, the one being edited, then R2, the donor drawn from the buffer.

Specification. Build a structural correspondence phi from E1 to E2 and
exchange what the corresponding entities mean.

  The map phi. Within each entity role type, list R1's entities most
  connected first (ties by id), list R2's the same way, and pair them off in
  that order. Pairs survive only when the two entities have equal degree --
  that is what "playing structurally equivalent roles" is checked as -- and
  when their label and sort actually differ, since swapping identical
  meanings is not an edit.

  The swap. Radicality selects how many surviving pairs are exchanged:

    k       = min(m - 1, floor(rho_hat * m)) + 1
    rho_hat = min(rho, rho_max) / rho_max,  rho_max is sampling.rho_max, default 8.0

  over the m surviving pairs, taken in id order. For each selected pair, the
  R1 entity takes the label and the sort of its R2 counterpart.

  Ids and topology of R1 are untouched: no entity is added, removed or
  renamed, and no relation changes. The graph keeps its shape and the
  entities change what they are about, which is the whole content of the
  edit. A, G and F stay those of R1. If no pair survives, the application is
  a no-op.

Worked example. R1 is a scheduling problem with server (input, sort
"machine", degree 3) and job (variable, sort "task", degree 3). R2 is a
chemical-kinetics problem with vessel (input, sort "reactor", degree 3) and
species (variable, sort "reagent", degree 3). At rho = 0.5, rho_hat = 0.0625
and k = floor(0.0625 * 2) + 1 = 1, so one pair moves: job takes the label and
sort of species. R' describes the same dependency graph, now read as reagents
flowing through a machine -- a reading under which conservation arguments
become available. The question to answer is still the original scheduling
question.
