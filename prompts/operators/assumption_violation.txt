Specification. Sample a subset A_flip of the assumption set A, uniformly and
without replacement, with

  |A_flip| = min(ceil(rho), |A|)

and replace each a in A_flip with its negation. A negated assumption keeps
its text and its load_bearing flag and carries "negated": true; it reads as
"not (<text>)". Nothing else moves: E, Rel, T, G and F are unchanged, and the
assumptions outside A_flip are unchanged.

Radicality for this operator is the *number* of assumptions inverted, not the
importance of any one of them. rho is rounded up, so any rho in (0, 1] flips
exactly one assumption and rho = 2.4 flips three; a rho larger than |A| flips
every assumption, and rho = 0 flips none and is reported as a no-op rather
than as an edit.

Choosing which to flip is deliberately uniform. Weighting the draw towards
load_bearing assumptions would duplicate foundational_negation, which is the
operator that targets the frame's axioms by how much rests on them.

Worked example. In "find the largest cap-set in F_3^n", A contains "n is
finite" (load_bearing) and "the ambient field has characteristic 3". At
rho = 0.8, |A_flip| = 1; if the draw picks the first, R' carries

  {"text": "n is finite", "load_bearing": true, "negated": true}

which reads as "not (n is finite)" -- the asymptotic version of the question,
whose lower bound was the target FunSearch improved. The goal, the entities
and the frame are untouched: the search is still expected to answer the
original finite-n question.

   count that rho asked for.
